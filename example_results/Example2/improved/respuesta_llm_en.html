LLM response

1. Summary of the compliance assessment:
- The project complies with the basic requirements for protection against falls at level differences, including the installation of barriers of suitable minimum height (110 cm for level differences greater than 600 cm), resistance to horizontal force in accordance with DB SE-AE, and limitation of guardrail openings to prevent the passage of a sphere 10 cm in diameter. It also provides visual and tactile differentiation for level differences of 55 cm or less, beginning at least 25 cm from the edge. The stairway has two handrails because its width exceeds 120 cm. No numerical non-compliance or significant omission is apparent from the information provided.

2. Compliance analysis:

2.1 Compliant sections:
- Minimum barrier height: A height of 110 cm is established for all façade protective barriers, standardising the design to withstand horizontal force. This complies with the minimum height of 90 cm for level differences ≤ 600 cm and 110 cm for greater level differences, under Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers (point 1).
- Height measurement: Height is measured vertically from floor level or from the pitch line on stairways to the top edge of the barrier, in accordance with the regulations (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers).
- Barrier strength: The barriers are stated to be designed to withstand the horizontal force established in DB SE-AE according to the area, complying with the strength and rigidity requirement (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.2 Strength).
- Visual and tactile differentiation: Level differences ≤ 55 cm will be made perceptible by visual and tactile differentiation beginning 25 cm from the edge, in accordance with the regulations (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.1 Protection against level differences).
- Limitation of stair-guardrail openings: Openings are limited to 10 cm, complying with the prohibition on openings through which a sphere 10 cm in diameter can pass, except for triangular openings between tread and riser with a maximum separation of 5 cm (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics, point b).
- Two handrails on a stairway wider than 120 cm: Two handrails are provided on each side, complying with the requirement for stairways wider than 120 cm (Checklist Cluster 8, handrail point).
- There are no level differences greater than 55 cm without protective barriers, complying with the obligation to install barriers at level differences greater than 55 cm (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.1 Protection against level differences, point 1).

2.2 Potentially non-compliant sections:
- The query does not explicitly state whether the barriers meet the requirements for not being readily climbable by children (absence of footholds or projections between heights of 30–50 cm and 50–80 cm). Evidence is therefore lacking to assess this requirement (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics, point a).
- It is not specified whether the distance between the lower edge of the guardrail and the stair pitch line is 5 cm or less for the permitted triangular openings, which is required for the exception to the 10 cm sphere rule (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics, point b).
- The exact horizontal force that the barriers must withstand according to the area is not specified, nor is it stated whether the design has been verified by specific calculations or tests in accordance with DB SE-AE (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.2 Strength).
- It is not stated whether barriers at stair voids less than 40 cm wide are at least 90 cm high, although this does not appear applicable in this case (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers).
- No information is provided on whether triangular openings exist between tread and riser with a maximum separation of 5 cm, other than the statement that openings are limited to 10 cm (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics, point b).
- It is not stated whether barrier height on the stairway has actually been measured correctly from the pitch line, although the project states that this method will be used (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers).
- It is not specified whether the two handrails comply with the technical characteristics for height, clearance, continuity and ease of grip established in the regulations (Checklist Cluster 8, handrails).

3. Recommendations:
- Verify and document that protective barriers meet the requirements for not being readily climbable by children by eliminating footholds or projections between heights of 30–50 cm and 50–80 cm, in accordance with the regulations (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics).
- Confirm that the distance between the lower edge of the guardrail and the stair pitch line does not exceed 5 cm for the permitted triangular openings, or modify the design to meet this condition (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.3 Construction characteristics).
- Provide calculations or tests demonstrating that the barriers withstand the horizontal force established in DB SE-AE for the relevant area (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2.2 Strength).
- Document that barrier height on stairways is measured from the pitch line and that the height meets the established minimum values (Section SUA 1 > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers).
- Verify that the two handrails comply with the technical height, clearance and continuity characteristics stated in the regulations (Checklist Cluster 8, handrails).
- Include drawings or details in the project demonstrating compliance with these points to facilitate assessment and certification.
