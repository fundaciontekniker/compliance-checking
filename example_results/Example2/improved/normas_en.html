Section SUA 1. Safety from the risk of falls > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers

Source: results/DBSUA/DBSUA_full.json

1 Protective barriers shall be at least 90 cm high where the difference in level they protect does not exceed 600 cm, and 110 cm high in all other cases, except for stair voids less than 40 cm wide, where the barrier shall be at least 90 cm high (see Figure 3.1). Height shall be measured vertically from floor level or, for stairways, from the pitch line defined by the step vertices to the top edge of the barrier. 3.2.2 Strength 1 Protective barriers shall have sufficient strength and rigidity to withstand the horizontal force established in Section 3.2.1 of Basic Document SE-AE, according to the area in which they are located. 3.2.3 Construction characteristics 1 In any area of Residential Housing buildings or nursery schools, and in public-use areas of Commercial or Public Assembly establishments, protective barriers, including those on stairways and ramps, shall be designed so that: a) They cannot be readily climbed by children; for this purpose: - Between heights of 30 cm and 50 cm above floor level or above the pitch line of a stairway, there shall be no footholds, including substantially horizontal projections extending more than 5 cm. - Between heights of 50 cm and 80 cm above floor level, there shall be no projections with a substantially horizontal surface deeper than 15 cm. b) They have no openings through which a sphere 10 cm in diameter can pass, except triangular openings formed by the tread and riser of the steps and the lower edge of the guardrail, provided that the distance between this edge and the stair pitch line does not exceed 5 cm (see Figure 3.2).

Section SUA 1. Safety from the risk of falls > 3 Level differences > 3.1 Protection against level differences

Source: results/DBSUA/DBSUA_full.json

1 To limit the risk of falls, protective barriers shall be provided at level differences, voids and openings (both horizontal and vertical), balconies, windows, etc. where the difference in level exceeds 55 cm, except where the construction arrangement makes a fall highly unlikely or where the barrier is incompatible with the intended use. 2 In public-use areas, level differences not exceeding 55 cm that could cause falls shall be made perceptible by means of visual and tactile differentiation. The differentiation shall begin at least 25 cm from the edge.
