1 On straight flights, the tread shall be at least 28 cm. The tread T and riser R shall satisfy the following relationship throughout the same stairway: 54 cm ≤ 2R + T ≤ 70 cm 2 Nosings are not permitted. The dimension of each tread shall be measured on each step in the direction of travel. 4 Tread measurement shall not include the vertical projection of the tread of the step above. 4.2.2 Flights 1 Except in the cases permitted in point 3 of Section 2 of this Section, each flight shall have at least 3 steps. The maximum rise of a flight shall be 2.25 m in public-use areas and whenever no lift is available as an alternative to the stairway, and 3.20 m in all other cases. 2 Flights may be straight, curved or mixed, except in hospital wards and intensive-treatment areas, nursery schools, and primary or secondary schools, where flights may only be straight. 3 Between two consecutive storeys served by the same stairway, all steps shall have the same riser height and all steps on straight flights shall have the same tread depth. Between two consecutive flights on different storeys, riser height shall not vary by more than 1 cm.

4.2.3 Landings 1 Landings between stair flights running in the same direction shall be at least as wide as the stairway and at least 1 m long, measured along their centreline. 2 Where there is a change of direction between two flights, the stairway width shall not be reduced across the landing (see Figure 4.4). The area defined by that width shall be free of obstacles and no door shall swing into it, except doors to unoccupied areas defined in Annex SI A of DB-SI. 3 In hospital wards or intensive-treatment areas, landings where the route requires a 180° turn shall be at least 1.60 m deep. 4 On storey landings of stairways in public-use areas, a strip of visually and tactilely contrasting flooring shall be provided at the start of the flights, in accordance with the characteristics specified in Section 2.2 of SUA 9. Such landings shall not contain corridors narrower than 1.20 m or doors located less than 40 cm from the first step of a flight. SUA 1. Safety from the risk of falls 4.2.4 Handrails 1 Stairways overcoming a height difference greater than 55 cm shall have a handrail on at least one side. Where their clear width exceeds 1.20 m, and where no lift is available as an alternative to the stairway, handrails shall be provided on both sides. 2 Intermediate handrails shall be provided where the flight width exceeds 4 m. The spacing between intermediate handrails shall not exceed 4 m, except on monumental stairways, where at least one intermediate handrail shall be provided. 3 On stairways in public-use areas, or where no lift is available as an alternative, the handrail shall extend 30 cm beyond the ends on at least one side. In healthcare use, the handrail shall be continuous throughout the entire route, including landings, and shall extend 30 cm beyond the ends on both sides.

Section SUA 1. Safety from the risk of falls > 3 Level differences > 3.2 Characteristics of protective barriers

Source: results/DBSUA/DBSUA_full.json

1 Protective barriers shall be at least 0.90 m high where the difference in level they protect does not exceed 6 m, and 1.10 m high in all other cases, except for stair voids less than 40 cm wide, where the barrier shall be at least 0.90 m high (see Figure 3.1). Height shall be measured vertically from floor level or, for stairways, from the pitch line defined by the step vertices to the top edge of the barrier. 3.2.2 Strength 1 Protective barriers shall have sufficient strength and rigidity to withstand the horizontal force established in Section 3.2.1 of Basic Document SE-AE, according to the area in which they are located. 3.2.3 Construction characteristics 1 In any area of Residential Housing buildings or nursery schools, and in public-use areas of Commercial or Public Assembly establishments, protective barriers, including those on stairways and ramps, shall be designed so that: a) They cannot be readily climbed by children; for this purpose: - Between heights of 30 cm and 50 cm above floor level or above the pitch line of a stairway, there shall be no footholds, including substantially horizontal projections extending more than 5 cm. - Between heights of 50 cm and 80 cm above floor level, there shall be no projections with a substantially horizontal surface deeper than 15 cm. b) They have no openings through which a sphere 10 cm in diameter can pass, except triangular openings formed by the tread and riser of the steps and the lower edge of the guardrail, provided that the distance between this edge and the stair pitch line does not exceed 5 cm (see Figure 3.2).

ANNEX III. TECHNICAL CONDITIONS FOR ACCESSIBILITY IN BUILDINGS > Article 10.– RESIDENTIAL BUILDINGS. > 10.2.– CONDITIONS INSIDE DWELLINGS. > 10.2.1.– COMMON CONDITIONS.

Source: results/anexo_decreto_68_2000/anexo_decreto_68_2000_full.json

The following conditions shall be met inside dwellings: – The clear opening or entrance door into the dwelling shall have a minimum width of 0.90 m. – The minimum clear opening dimension of doors inside the dwelling shall be 0.80 m. – The corridor shall have a minimum width at all points of 0.95 m. – In dwellings extending over more than one storey, technical and design provisions shall be made to facilitate the possible installation of a lift. – External window and door units shall be operable, or accessibility for their maintenance and cleaning shall be ensured. – Where the dwelling has a terrace, it shall be constructed without steep gradients and the drains shall be fitted with grilles made of slip-resistant material even when wet. The grilles shall have maximum openings of 1 cm and a grid arrangement that prevents tripping by people using walking sticks or wheelchairs. – Where a door-entry system is installed, it shall be a video door-entry system.
