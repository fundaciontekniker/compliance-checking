Checklists

Cluster 8

- [ ] Protective barriers shall be at least 90 cm high where they protect level differences up to 600 cm, and 110 cm high where they protect greater level differences, except for stair voids less than 40 cm wide, where the minimum height shall be 90 cm.  
- [ ] Barrier height shall be measured vertically from floor level or from the pitch line of the stairway to the top edge of the barrier.  
- [ ] Barriers shall withstand the horizontal force established in DB SE-AE according to the area in which they are located.  
- [ ] In Residential Housing buildings, nursery schools, and public areas of Commercial or Public Assembly establishments, barriers (including those on stairways and ramps) shall be designed to:  
  - [ ] Not be readily climbable by children:  
    - [ ] Between 30 and 50 cm in height, there shall be no footholds or horizontal projections extending more than 5 cm.  
    - [ ] Between 50 and 80 cm in height, there shall be no projections with a horizontal surface deeper than 15 cm.  
  - [ ] Have no openings through which a sphere 10 cm in diameter can pass, except triangular openings between tread and riser with a maximum separation of 5 cm from the pitch line.  
- [ ] In public areas of uses other than those above, barriers shall prevent the passage of a sphere 15 cm in diameter.  
- [ ] Barriers in front of rows of fixed seats may have their height reduced to 70 cm if they incorporate a horizontal element at least 50 cm wide located at a height of 50 cm, and shall simultaneously withstand a horizontal force of 3 kN/m at the top edge and a uniformly distributed vertical force of 1 kN/m at the outer edge.  
- [ ] Restricted-use stairways:  
  - [ ] Minimum flight width: 80 cm.  
  - [ ] Maximum riser height: 20 cm.  
  - [ ] Minimum tread depth: 22 cm (measured in the direction of travel).  
  - [ ] On curved stairways, the minimum tread shall be 5 cm on the narrow side and the maximum 44 cm on the wide side.  
  - [ ] Split landings with steps at 45° and open-riser steps with a minimum overlap of 2.5 cm are permitted.  
  - [ ] Guardrails shall be provided on open sides.  
- [ ] General-use stairways:  
  - [ ] On straight flights, minimum tread depth: 28 cm.  
  - [ ] Riser height between 13 cm and 18.5 cm, except in public areas or where there is no lift, where the maximum shall be 17.5 cm.  
  - [ ] The dimensional relationship shall satisfy: 54 cm ≤ 2R + T ≤ 70 cm (R = riser, T = tread).  
  - [ ] Nosings are not permitted.  
  - [ ] On stairways for upward evacuation or without an alternative accessible route, risers shall be vertical or inclined by no more than 15°.  
  - [ ] On curved flights, the minimum tread shall be 28 cm at 50 cm from the inner edge and the maximum 44 cm at the outer edge.  
  - [ ] Each flight shall have at least 3 steps.  
  - [ ] Maximum rise per flight: 225 cm in public areas or where there is no lift, and 320 cm in other cases.  
  - [ ] Flights may be straight, curved or mixed, except in hospital wards, nursery schools and primary/secondary schools, where they shall be straight only.  
  - [ ] Steps on straight flights shall have uniform tread and riser dimensions; between consecutive flights, riser height shall not vary by more than 1 cm.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear width according to the DB-SI table, free of obstacles, measured between walls or barriers without deducting handrails that project no more than 12 cm.  
  - [ ] On curved flights, areas with a tread depth of less than 17 cm shall be excluded when measuring clear width.  
- [ ] Stair landings:  
  - [ ] Landings between flights running in the same direction shall be as wide as the stairway and at least 100 cm long.  
  - [ ] At changes of direction, the width shall not be reduced and the area shall be free of obstacles and doors swinging into the turning space (except in unoccupied areas).  
  - [ ] In hospital wards or intensive-treatment areas, landings involving a 180° turn shall be at least 160 cm deep.  
  - [ ] On storey landings in public areas, a strip of visually and tactilely contrasting flooring shall be provided at the start of flights.  
  - [ ] There shall be no corridors narrower than 120 cm or doors less than 40 cm from the first step.  
- [ ] Handrails on stairways:  
  - [ ] Stairways overcoming a level difference of more than 55 cm shall have a handrail on at least one side.  
  - [ ] If the clear width exceeds 120 cm or there is no lift, handrails shall be provided on both sides.  
  - [ ] Intermediate handrails shall be provided where the width exceeds 400 cm, with a maximum spacing of 400 cm between them.  
  - [ ] In public areas or where there is no lift, the handrail shall extend 30 cm beyond the ends on at least one side.  
  - [ ] In healthcare use, the handrail shall be continuous throughout the route and extend 30 cm beyond the ends on both sides.  
  - [ ] Handrail height between 90 and 110 cm.  
  - [ ] In nursery and primary schools, an additional handrail shall be provided at a height of between 65 and 75 cm.  
  - [ ] Handrails shall be sturdy, easy to grip, separated from the wall by at least 4 cm, and have a fixing system that does not obstruct the continuous passage of the hand.  
- [ ] In Residential Housing buildings, transparent glazing more than 600 cm above ground level shall meet the following requirements:  
  - [ ] The entire exterior surface of the glazing shall be within a radius of 85 cm from an openable edge located no more than 130 cm above floor level.  
  - [ ] Reversible glazing shall have a device that keeps it locked in the reversed position during cleaning.  
- [ ] In circulation areas:  
  - [ ] Minimum clear headroom: 210 cm in restricted-use areas and 220 cm in other areas.  
  - [ ] At door thresholds, minimum clear height: 200 cm.  
  - [ ] Fixed projecting elements on façades above circulation areas shall be at least 220 cm high.  
  - [ ] Walls shall not have cantilevered projecting elements extending more than 15 cm between heights of 15 and 220 cm where they pose an impact risk.  
  - [ ] Cantilevered elements with a height below 200 cm (landings, stair flights or ramp flights) shall have fixed elements restricting access and enabling detection with canes.  
- [ ] Doors in corridors less than 250 cm wide (except restricted-use and unoccupied areas):  
  - [ ] The swing of the door leaf shall not encroach on the corridor.  
  - [ ] In corridors wider than 250 cm, the door swing shall not encroach on the width required for evacuation.  
- [ ] Swing doors between circulation areas shall have transparent or translucent sections visible between 70 and 150 cm above floor level.  
- [ ] Industrial, commercial and garage doors and gates shall comply with the applicable specific regulations and bear CE marking.  
- [ ] Automatic pedestrian doors shall comply with the applicable specific regulations and bear CE marking.  
- [ ] Large glazed surfaces that could be mistaken for doors (excluding those inside dwellings) shall have contrasting visual markings:  
  - [ ] Lower marking between 85 and 110 cm above floor level.  
  - [ ] Upper marking between 150 and 170 cm above floor level.  
  - [ ] Markings are not required if mullions are spaced no more than 60 cm apart or if a transom is provided at the lower height stated above.  
- [ ] Glass doors without identifying elements (frames or handles) shall have markings in accordance with the above.  
- [ ] Manually operated sliding doors shall have a minimum distance of 20 cm between the nearest fixed object and the opening/closing mechanism to prevent trapping.  
- [ ] Automatic opening and closing elements shall have suitable protective devices.  
- [ ] Grandstands for standing spectators:  
  - [ ] Maximum gradient: 50%.  
  - [ ] Maximum row length with access from both ends: 2000 cm; with access from one end: 1000 cm.  
  - [ ] Clear aisle width in accordance with DB-SI.  
  - [ ] Maximum difference in level between any row and an exit: 400 cm.  
  - [ ] In grandstands with more than 5 rows and a gradient greater than 6%, a continuous barrier or crush barrier at least 110 cm high shall be installed in front of the first row, together with additional barriers in accordance with the table based on the gradient.  
  - [ ] Barriers shall withstand a horizontal force of 5 kN/m at the top edge.  
  - [ ] No more than 2 openings shall be aligned in consecutive rows; the line of openings shall form an angle of less than 60° with the barriers.  
- [ ] Swimming pools where children's access is not controlled shall have barriers preventing access except at access points fitted with openable elements having closing and locking devices.  
- [ ] Swimming-pool barriers shall be at least 120 cm high, withstand a horizontal force of 0.5 kN/m, and comply with the construction requirements for general barriers (3.2.3).  
- [ ] Maximum pool depth: 50 cm in children's pools and 300 cm in other pools, with areas shallower than 140 cm.  
- [ ] Points deeper than 140 cm and the maximum and minimum depths shall be marked on the walls and pool surround, visible from both inside and outside the pool.  
- [ ] Maximum gradients at changes in depth: 6% in children's pools; 10% up to a depth of 140 cm and 35% elsewhere in recreational/multipurpose pools.  
- [ ] Openings in the pool shell shall be protected by grilles or devices preventing entrapment.  
- [ ] The pool-floor material in areas no deeper than 150 cm shall be Class 3 slip-resistant.  
- [ ] The internal pool lining shall be light-coloured so that the bottom can be seen.  
- [ ] The deck or surround around the pool shall have Class 3 slip-resistant paving, be at least 120 cm wide, and prevent ponding.  
- [ ] Pool ladders (except in children's pools) shall extend at least 100 cm below the water or to within 30 cm of the pool floor.  
- [ ] Ladders shall be located near corners and changes in gradient, with a maximum spacing of 1500 cm; their steps shall be slip-resistant, have no sharp edges, and not project beyond the plane of the wall.  
- [ ] Wheelchair manoeuvring spaces for passing through doors:  
  - [ ] An obstacle-free space for turning shall be provided according to the approach direction and direction of opening.  
  - [ ] An obstacle-free space shall be provided on the other side of the door.  
  - [ ] Opening/closing mechanisms (latches and pull handles) shall be located in an accessible area and be operable without obstruction.  
- [ ] Transfers for wheelchair users:  
  - [ ] Clear frontal space of between 120 and 200 cm in front of the element for forward transfers.  
  - [ ] Minimum lateral space of 80 cm for side transfers.  
  - [ ] Recommended minimum clear turning space: 180 cm; accepted minimum: 150 cm.  
  - [ ] The height of toilets, beds, seats and body supports shall be 45 cm (wheelchair seat height).  
- [ ] Visual information elements:  
  - [ ] For standing persons, located between 105 and 195 cm above floor level (average 150 cm).  
  - [ ] For seated persons or wheelchair users, between 60 and 145 cm above floor level.  
- [ ] Stairways in the urban environment:  
  - [ ] Accessible design and layout for persons with reduced mobility.  
  - [ ] Straight alignment; fan-shaped stairways are permitted if the tread is not less than 35 cm at any point.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear width: 200 cm.  
  - [ ] Two handrails on both sides; if the width exceeds 240 cm, intermediate handrails shall be provided.  
  - [ ] Handrails shall extend across landings and intermediate landings.  
  - [ ] Slip-resistant tread, without projections over the riser, minimum depth 35 cm.  
  - [ ] Maximum riser height: 15 cm.  
  - [ ] Between 3 and 12 steps per flight, without overlapping.  
  - [ ] The soffit of the lowest flight shall be enclosed up to a height of 220 cm.  
  - [ ] Intermediate landings shall be at least 150 cm long.  
  - [ ] Maximum gradient for water drainage: 1.5%.  
  - [ ] Tactile signage at stairway entrances in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] Ramps in the urban environment:  
  - [ ] Minimum width: 200 cm.  
  - [ ] Maximum longitudinal gradient: 8%.  
  - [ ] Maximum crossfall: 1.5%.  
  - [ ] Maximum flight length without landings: 1000 cm.  
  - [ ] Intermediate landings shall be at least 200 cm long.  
  - [ ] Access areas shall provide space in which a circle 180 cm in diameter can be inscribed.  
  - [ ] Handrails on both sides with the characteristics specified in point 3.10 of the Annex.  
  - [ ] Sides protected by raised kerbs projecting at least 5 cm above the paving.  
  - [ ] Slip-resistant flooring.  
  - [ ] Marking by means of a warning strip in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] External entrance doors:  
  - [ ] Clear space on both sides in which an obstacle-free circle 180 cm in diameter can be inscribed (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Minimum opening angle: 90°.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear opening width: 90 cm (120 cm for automatic opening).  
  - [ ] For double-leaf doors, the normally used leaf shall provide a minimum clear width of 90 cm.  
  - [ ] Automatic closing mechanisms shall require an opening force of no more than 30 N.  
  - [ ] Draught-lobby doors shall maintain these conditions.  
- [ ] Emergency exits:  
  - [ ] Doors with two opening bars at heights of 90 cm and 20 cm, operable by simple pressure.  
- [ ] Glazed doors:  
  - [ ] Safety glass.  
  - [ ] Protective plinth 40 cm high.  
  - [ ] Two horizontal warning bands 20 cm wide, at heights of 90 cm and 150 cm above the floor.  
  - [ ] If the surrounding wall is also glazed, the door shall be highlighted to prevent visual confusion.  
- [ ] Turnstiles, barriers or other access-control elements:  
  - [ ] Alternative openings at least 90 cm wide and no more than 1000 cm apart.  
  - [ ] Operating systems accessible to persons with manipulation difficulties, located between 90 and 120 cm above floor level.  
- [ ] Entrance halls:  
  - [ ] Regular shapes, without unnecessary pillars or columns, with space to inscribe an obstacle-free circle 180 cm in diameter (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Permanent lighting without shadows, at least 300 lux, and without glare.  
  - [ ] Good colour contrast between floors and walls.  
  - [ ] Hard, slip-resistant, continuous and level flooring.  
  - [ ] Switches that are easy to locate, with colour contrast and an indicator light, accessible between heights of 90 and 120 cm.  
  - [ ] Furniture, counters and service windows in accordance with the regulations.  
  - [ ] Plans and models available in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] Internal routes:  
  - [ ] Corners and edges without sharp arrises, with warning finishes.  
  - [ ] Main routes free of obstacles within a prism 220 cm high and 180 cm wide (150 cm wide in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Hard, slip-resistant, continuous and level flooring, without folds or wrinkles.  
  - [ ] Directional guidance strips in accordance with Annex IV.  
  - [ ] In large facilities with pedestrian routes longer than 10000 cm, wheelchairs or other means of transport shall be available for persons with reduced mobility, at a rate of 1 for every 100 persons of maximum occupancy.  
- [ ] Corridors:  
  - [ ] Minimum clear passage width: 180 cm in main corridors and 150 cm in common areas of dwellings.  
  - [ ] Secondary corridors and access routes to facilities, storage rooms, garages, etc.: at least 120 cm.  
  - [ ] Meeting and turning areas measuring 150 x 150 cm, spaced no more than 1800 cm apart and always provided at the beginning and end of the corridor.  
  - [ ] Furniture in corridors shall be placed on one side only.  
  - [ ] Suitable lighting in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] Internal doors:  
  - [ ] Comply with the requirements for external doors (minimum clear passage width of 90 cm).  
  - [ ] Turning diameters of 120 cm in corridors of that width.  
  - [ ] Latches and pull handles shall not project more than 7 cm from the plane of the door.  
  - [ ] Colour contrast between doors and walls in accordance with Annex IV.  
  - [ ] Windows or vision panels in doors shall allow viewing by persons of short stature or wheelchair users, with vision panels at 110 cm or extending down to that height.  
- [ ] Windows in horizontal circulation routes:  
  - [ ] Operating and locking mechanisms located between 80 and 110 cm above floor level, without obstacles.  
  - [ ] Opening shall not encroach on the corridor below a height of 220 cm.  
- [ ] Internal stairways:  
  - [ ] No isolated steps.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear headroom beneath stairways: 220 cm.  
  - [ ] With risers and without nosings.  
  - [ ] No overlapping steps.  
  - [ ] Soffit of the lowest flight enclosed up to 220 cm.  
  - [ ] Handrails on both sides if the width exceeds 120 cm, along the entire usable route across landings and intermediate landings.  
  - [ ] Intermediate handrails if the width exceeds 240 cm.  
  - [ ] Handrails with the characteristics specified in point 5.3.3.  
  - [ ] Tactile signage at entrances in accordance with Annex IV.  
  - [ ] Suitable lighting in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] Internal ramps:  
  - [ ] Minimum width 180 cm (100 cm in dwellings, with 150 x 150 cm landings and a maximum distance of 1000 cm between them).  
  - [ ] No stairway shall be located less than 300 cm beyond the end of a downward-leading ramp.  
  - [ ] Maximum gradient: 10% for flights no longer than 300 cm and 8% for other flights; 6% is recommended.  
  - [ ] Maximum flight length without landings: 1000 cm.  
  - [ ] Intermediate landings shall be at least 180 cm long (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Access areas with surfaces in which a circle 180 cm in diameter can be inscribed (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Slip-resistant flooring.  
  - [ ] Raised side kerbs projecting at least 5 cm above the paving.  
  - [ ] Handrails on both sides if the flight exceeds 200 cm, with the characteristics specified in point 5.3.3.  
- [ ] Lifts:  
  - [ ] Access platform with clear space for a circle 180 cm in diameter (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Illuminance of at least 100 lux at floor level and a 150 x 150 cm warning strip with contrasting texture and colour in front of the door.  
  - [ ] Floor indicators at 150 cm, in raised lettering and Braille, preferably on the right-hand side.  
  - [ ] Audible and visual indicators for calls and arrival.  
  - [ ] Call buttons between 90 and 120 cm above floor level, on the same side on every storey, with good lighting and colour contrast.  
  - [ ] Control panel with legible characters, raised lettering and Braille, and good contrast.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear car dimensions: depth 140 cm, width 110 cm.  
  - [ ] In groups of lifts, at least one shall be accessible; the others shall have a minimum depth of 125 cm and width of 100 cm.  
  - [ ] If entry and exit are in different directions, minimum dimensions shall be 180 x 150 cm (150 x 150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Level difference between car and landing ≤ 2 cm; gap ≤ 3.5 cm.  
  - [ ] Hard, slip-resistant, smooth and fixed flooring.  
  - [ ] Control panel beside the main door, with buttons between 90 and 120 cm above floor level and at least 40 cm from walls or doors.  
  - [ ] Push-operated buttons, illuminated internally when pressed, with a contrasting colour.  
  - [ ] Buttons with raised numbers and Braille.  
  - [ ] Alarm and stop buttons differentiated by location and colour.  
  - [ ] Illuminated position indicator showing the floor when stopped and in transit.  
  - [ ] Uniform interior illuminance of at least 100 lux at floor level.  
  - [ ] Continuous interior handrail at 90 ± 5 cm, ergonomic and separated 4 cm from the walls.  
  - [ ] Self-contained emergency lighting supply.  
  - [ ] Laminated safety mirror opposite the door to facilitate manoeuvring.  
  - [ ] If a seating area is provided, the seat shall be 45 cm high, folding and self-returning.  
  - [ ] Intercom meeting the same location, visibility and lighting requirements as the control panels.  
- [ ] Escalators:  
  - [ ] Minimum clear width: 100 cm.  
  - [ ] Side guards with handrails at 90.5 cm, extending 45 cm beyond the ends.  
  - [ ] At least 2 level steps at the entrance and exit.  
- [ ] Moving walkways:  
  - [ ] Minimum clear width: 100 cm.  
  - [ ] Entrance and exit areas with a horizontal transition of at least 150 cm.  
  - [ ] Side guards with handrails at 90.5 cm, extending 45 cm beyond the ends.  
  - [ ] Inclined moving walkways shall comply with ramp requirements except for width.  
  - [ ] Moving walkways considered evacuation routes shall comply with corridor or ramp regulations.  
- [ ] Vertical lifting platforms:  
  - [ ] Overcome level differences permitted by current regulations.  
  - [ ] Clear space at boarding and alighting points for a circle 180 cm in diameter (150 cm in dwellings).  
  - [ ] Push-button controls on the platform and at boarding areas between 90 and 120 cm above floor level, easy to operate, with an instruction panel for independent use.  
  - [ ] Minimum dimensions 140 x 110 cm, with a protective barrier and stops to prevent a wheelchair from sliding.  
  - [ ] Minimum load capacity 250 kg; maximum speed 10 cm/s in accordance with the regulations.  
  - [ ] Doors 90 cm wide.  
- [ ] Inclined lifting platforms (only in refurbishment where no other option exists):  
  - [ ] Boarding and alighting areas equivalent to those for vertical platforms.  
  - [ ] Minimum dimensions 125 x 100 cm, with a protective barrier and wheelchair stops.  
  - [ ] Minimum load capacity 250 kg; maximum speed 10 cm/s in accordance with the regulations.  
  - [ ] Doors 80 cm wide.  
- [ ] Recreational swimming pools in public-assembly facilities:  
  - [ ] Pool surround free of obstructions, at least 180 cm wide, with a slight outward gradient ≤ 2%.  
  - [ ] Slip-resistant and waterproof flooring.  
  - [ ] One or more assistive devices (hoist) for persons with reduced mobility.  
  - [ ] Stairways at least 120 cm wide, with slip-resistant treads at least 30 cm deep and risers no higher than 16 cm.  
  - [ ] Guardrails and handrails on both sides and at two heights (90 and 70 cm), continuous with those in the pool.  
  - [ ] Footbaths accessible to wheelchairs and an alternative passage for cane users.  
  - [ ] Perimeter marking around the pool with a 50 cm wide strip of contrasting texture and colour for persons with severe visual impairment.  
- [ ] Fountains and drinking fountains as street furniture:  
  - [ ] No changes in level that prevent approach.  
  - [ ] Drainage grilles slip-resistant in both dry and wet conditions, with maximum openings of 2.5 x 2.5 cm and designed to prevent blockages.  
  - [ ] Manual control no higher than 90 cm and operable by persons with difficulties.  
- [ ] Benches in public open spaces:  
  - [ ] Seat height between 40 and 50 cm.  
  - [ ] Backrest and armrests, the latter between 20 and 25 cm above the seat.  
  - [ ] Maximum distance between benches: 5000 cm.  
  - [ ] Other types of bench are permitted only as supplementary seating and shall meet ergonomic conditions that facilitate sitting down and standing up.  
- [ ] Counters and service windows in open spaces:  
  - [ ] Maximum height: 110 cm.  
  - [ ] Section at least 120 cm long at a height of 80 cm.  
  - [ ] Unobstructed space beneath, 70 cm high and 50 cm deep.  
- [ ] Inside dwellings:  
  - [ ] Entrance door with two peepholes, the lower one at 110 cm or below.  
  - [ ] Clear space on both sides of the door for a turning circle 180 cm in diameter.  
  - [ ] Entrance and internal doors with a minimum clear opening of 90 cm.  
  - [ ] Doors operable by persons with manipulation difficulties.  
  - [ ] Toilet-room doors shall never open inwards and may be sliding doors.  
  - [ ] Corridors with a minimum width of 95 cm.  
  - [ ] In multi-storey dwellings, the design shall facilitate the possible installation of a lifting device.  
  - [ ] External joinery shall be openable or accessible for maintenance and cleaning.  
  - [ ] Terraces without steep gradients or steps, with parapets/guardrails designed to take account of the field of vision of a seated person.  
  - [ ] Drains with slip-resistant grilles, maximum openings of 1 cm, arranged to prevent canes or wheelchairs from catching.  
  - [ ] The entryphone shall be a video intercom.  
- [ ] Dwellings for wheelchair users or persons with reduced mobility:  
  - [ ] Clear space in front of doors and work areas (food preparation and sink) in which an obstacle-free circle 180 cm in diameter can be inscribed.  
  - [ ] Minimum clear space between furniture: 90 cm.  
  - [ ] Sink rim at 80 cm, clear height beneath the sink of 65 cm, and single-lever tap.  
  - [ ] Hob and worktop at a height of 80 cm and maximum depth of 65 cm.  
  - [ ] Plinth of base units 20 cm high and recessed 16 cm.  
  - [ ] Front-loading appliances, with doors opening sideways and preferably built in.  
  - [ ] Bedrooms: at least one double bedroom, with a minimum clear lateral space of 90 cm on both sides of the bed and 110 cm at the foot, and space for a 180 cm circle in front of the door.  
  - [ ] Living rooms with a minimum clear passage of 90 cm and clear space for a circle 180 cm in diameter.

  - [ ] On panels containing a large amount of information, nearby primary indicators shall direct users to close information points.

Cluster 9

- [ ] The project shall identify all routes with a gradient greater than 4% as ramps, except restricted-use routes and vehicle circulation routes in car parks that are not accessible to pedestrians.  
- [ ] Ramps shall have a maximum gradient of:  
  - 12% in general.  
  - For accessible routes:  
    - ≤10% if length < 300 cm.  
    - ≤8% if length < 600 cm.  
    - ≤6% in all other cases.  
  - For ramps in car parks carrying both vehicles and pedestrians, where the ramp is not accessible: ≤16%.  
- [ ] The crossfall of ramps on accessible routes shall be no more than 2%.  
- [ ] Ramp flights shall have a maximum length of:  
  - 1500 cm in general.  
  - 900 cm for accessible routes.  
  - No limit for ramps in car parks carrying both vehicles and pedestrians.  
- [ ] The minimum clear width of the ramp shall be that specified for stairways in Table 4.1 of DB-SI, free of obstacles and measured between walls or protective barriers, without deducting handrails that project no more than 12 cm.  
- [ ] On accessible routes, ramp flights shall be straight or have a radius of curvature ≥ 3000 cm, with a minimum width of 120 cm.  
- [ ] On accessible routes, a horizontal surface (landing) shall be provided at the beginning and end of every ramp flight, with a minimum length of 120 cm in the direction of the ramp.  
- [ ] Landings between ramp flights running in the same direction shall be at least as wide as the ramp and at least 150 cm long, measured along their centreline.  
- [ ] At changes of direction between ramp flights:  
  - The width shall not be reduced across the landing.  
  - The area defined by that width shall be free of obstacles.  
  - No door shall swing into the turning area, except in unoccupied areas.  
- [ ] There shall be no corridors narrower than 120 cm or doors located less than 40 cm from the start of a ramp flight; where the ramp is accessible, this distance shall be at least 150 cm.  
- [ ] Ramps overcoming a height difference greater than 55 cm and having a gradient ≥ 6% shall have a continuous handrail on at least one side.  
- [ ] Ramps on accessible routes with a gradient ≥ 6% and a height difference greater than 18.5 cm shall have:  
  - Continuous handrails on both sides, including landings.  
  - A kerb or lateral protection at least 10 cm high at free edges.  
  - Handrails extending horizontally at least 30 cm beyond both ends of flights longer than 300 cm.  
- [ ] Handrail height shall be between 90 and 110 cm.  
- [ ] In nursery schools, primary education centres and accessible ramps, a second handrail shall be provided at a height of between 65 and 75 cm.  
- [ ] The handrail shall be sturdy and easy to grip, separated from the wall by at least 4 cm, and have a fixing system that does not obstruct the continuous passage of the hand.  
- [ ] Stepped aisles providing access to seating in grandstands shall have constant riser heights and alternating tread dimensions so that spectator rows can be accessed at the same level.  
- [ ] The width of stepped aisles shall comply with the evacuation requirements in Section 4 of Section SI 3 of DB-SI.  
- [ ] In car parks:  
  - An access and waiting area at least 450 cm deep and with a maximum gradient of 5% shall be provided.  
  - Pedestrian routes provided on vehicle ramps shall be at least 80 cm wide and protected by a barrier at least 80 cm high, or by paving at a higher level with the level difference complying with SUA 1, Section 3.1.  
  - In car parks with more than 200 vehicles or an area greater than 5000 m², pedestrian routes in public areas shall be identified by differentiated paving (paint, relief or raised level).  
  - If the level difference of the differentiated paving exceeds 55 cm, it shall be protected in accordance with SUA 1, Section 3.2.  
  - In front of doors connecting car parks to other areas, barriers shall be installed at a minimum distance of 120 cm and a minimum height of 80 cm.  
- [ ] Signage in car parks and traffic routes shall include:  
  - Direction of travel and exits.  
  - Maximum speed of 20 km/h.  
  - Traffic areas and pedestrian crossings.  
  - Clearance gauges and restricted heights in car parks accessible to heavy transport.  
  - Storage and loading/unloading areas marked and delimited by road markings or paint.  
  - Devices at vehicle entrances to external roads that alert drivers to pedestrians.  
- [ ] The project shall provide at least one accessible route connecting the building's main entrance with the public highway and external common areas (car parks, gardens, swimming pools, sports areas, etc.).  
- [ ] In residential buildings:  
  - If more than two storeys are overcome from the accessible main entrance to dwellings or communal areas, or if there are more than 12 dwellings without an accessible entrance, an accessible lift or accessible ramp shall connect all occupied storeys with the accessible entrance.  
  - In other cases, dimensional and structural provision shall be made for the future installation of an accessible lift.  
  - Storeys containing wheelchair-accessible dwellings shall have an accessible lift or ramp connecting them with the accessible entrance and with associated elements (storage rooms, parking spaces, communal rooms, etc.).  
- [ ] In buildings of other uses:  
  - If more than two storeys are overcome from the accessible entrance to an occupied storey, or if the total usable floor area (excluding unoccupied areas without an accessible entrance) exceeds 200 m², an accessible lift or ramp shall connect those storeys with the accessible entrance.  
  - Storeys with public-use areas exceeding 100 m² or with accessible elements (parking spaces, sanitary facilities, accessible accommodation, etc.) shall have an accessible lift or ramp connecting them with the accessible entrance.  
- [ ] On each storey, an accessible route shall connect the accessible access point to the storey with:  
  - Communal or public-use areas.  
  - Evacuation origins (except unoccupied areas).  
  - Accessible elements (parking spaces, accessible toilets, reserved spaces, accessible accommodation, accessible service points, etc.).  
- [ ] The number of dwellings accessible to wheelchair users and persons with hearing disabilities shall comply with the applicable regulations.  
- [ ] In public residential-use establishments, accessible accommodation shall be provided in the number specified in Table 1.1 (minimum 8, plus one for every additional 50 accommodation units or fraction above 250).  
- [ ] In residential buildings with their own car park, one accessible parking space shall be provided for each wheelchair-accessible dwelling.  
- [ ] In other uses with their own car park and a built area greater than 100 m², accessible spaces shall be provided as follows:  
  - Public residential use: one accessible space per accessible accommodation unit.  
  - Commercial, public assembly or public car park: one accessible space for every 33 spaces or fraction thereof.  
  - Other uses: one accessible space for every 50 spaces or fraction thereof up to 200 spaces, and one additional space for every additional 100 spaces or fraction thereof.  
- [ ] In all cases, there shall be at least one accessible parking space for each space reserved for wheelchair users.  
- [ ] In spaces with fixed public seating (auditoriums, cinemas, assembly halls, performance venues, etc.):  
  - One space reserved for wheelchair users for every 100 seats or fraction thereof.  
  - In spaces with more than 50 fixed seats and an auditory component, one reserved space for persons with hearing disabilities for every 50 seats or fraction thereof.  
- [ ] In waiting areas with fixed seating, one space reserved for wheelchair users for every 100 seats or fraction thereof.  
- [ ] Swimming pools open to the public, those in public residential establishments with accessible accommodation, and those in buildings with accessible dwellings shall have an adapted means of entry (hoist or equivalent), except children's pools.  
- [ ] Where toilets or changing rooms are required, at least the following shall be provided:  
  - One accessible toilet for every 10 toilet units or fraction thereof; it may be unisex.  
  - In each changing room, one accessible cubicle, one accessible toilet and one accessible shower for every 10 units or fraction thereof. If there are no individual cubicles, at least one accessible cubicle shall be provided.  
- [ ] Fixed furniture in public-service areas shall include at least one accessible service point or an accessible call point for requesting assistance.  
- [ ] Switches, intercommunication devices and alarm buttons shall be accessible, except in dwellings and unoccupied areas.  
- [ ] Accessible entrances, accessible routes, accessible parking spaces and accessible sanitary facilities shall be marked with the International Symbol of Access (ISA), supplemented by directional arrows where appropriate.  
- [ ] Accessible lifts shall be marked with the ISA and shall indicate the floor number in Braille and raised lettering, at a height between 80 and 120 cm, on the right-hand jamb when exiting.  
- [ ] General-use sanitary facilities shall be marked with standardised pictograms in raised relief and colour contrast, at a height between 80 and 120 cm, beside the right-hand door frame in the direction of entry.  
- [ ] Visual and tactile warning strips shall contrast in colour with the paving, with a relief of 3 ± 0.1 cm indoors and 5 ± 0.1 cm outdoors.  
- [ ] Strips marking the start of stairways shall be 80 cm long in the direction of travel, as wide as the route, and have grooves perpendicular to the stair axis.  
- [ ] Strips marking accessible routes to accessible call or service points shall have grooves parallel to the direction of travel and be 40 cm wide.  
- [ ] On public or private pedestrian routes for communal use:  
  - Minimum obstacle-free width of 200 cm, except in developments with ≤ 12 dwellings/ha, where it may be reduced to 150 cm if 180 x 180 cm passing and turning areas are installed at intervals of no more than 2000 cm and are visible from one another.  
  - Minimum clear headroom of 220 cm.  
  - Longitudinal gradient ≤ 6%.  
  - Crossfall ≤ 2%; 1.5% is recommended.  
  - Kerbs on pavements with a maximum height of 12 cm, a rounded edge with a maximum radius of 1 cm, or a 2 cm chamfer.  
- [ ] Pedestrian-route paving shall be slip-resistant and have no raised joints between units; soft surfaces shall be sufficiently compacted to prevent assistive devices from shifting or sinking.  
- [ ] To indicate level differences, depressions and changes in level, warning strips at least 100 cm wide shall be installed, with a different and contrasting texture and colour, perpendicular to the direction of travel and extending across the full width of the route.  
- [ ] Grilles and access covers on pedestrian routes shall be flush with the paving and slip-resistant, with maximum openings of 1 x 1 cm if they encroach on the minimum route width, or 2.5 x 2.5 cm otherwise.  
- [ ] Tree pits on pedestrian routes shall be covered by flush elements without gaps or deformation; if gridded, they shall comply with the opening requirements specified for grilles.  
- [ ] Vehicle crossings shall be designed so that pedestrian routes crossing them do not exceed the longitudinal and transverse gradients specified for pedestrian routes.  
- [ ] Where the gradient and distance between the carriageway and parking area cannot be met, a minimum pavement width of 150 cm with permissible gradients shall be guaranteed.  
- [ ] On a 150 cm pavement where the crossing cannot be formed, the solution shall be to lower the kerb.  
- [ ] In parks, gardens, squares and public open spaces:  
  - Paths or trails at least 200 cm wide, paved with non-deformable, slip-resistant material whose texture and colour contrast with the surroundings.  
  - All level differences shall be overcome by accessible pedestrian routes or ramps; supplementary stairways may also be installed.  
  - Continuous protective elements shall be installed on raised platforms or level differences greater than 40 cm.  
  - Tree planting shall not allow branches or trunks to encroach on pedestrian routes below a height of 220 cm.  
  - Street furniture should be aligned along the route to aid orientation for persons with visual impairments.  
  - Tactile or tactile-audible plans shall be provided in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] Escalators, moving walkways and lifts in public open spaces shall comply with the accessibility and signage requirements of this Annex and the technical requirements of Annex III.  
- [ ] In light-vehicle parking areas on roads or in open spaces around buildings:  
  - At least one accessible parking space for every 40 spaces or fraction thereof shall be permanently reserved for vehicles used by persons with reduced mobility.  
  - Reserved spaces shall be close to pedestrian routes and entrances to buildings and public services.  
  - They shall be marked with the International Symbol of Access on both vertical and horizontal planes and with signs prohibiting use by other vehicles.  
  - Horizontal markings shall be slip-resistant.  
  - On public roads, the distance between reserved spaces along a pedestrian route shall not exceed 250 m.  
  - Safe access for wheelchair users from the reserved space to the pedestrian route or pavement shall be ensured.  
  - Where a reserved space is close to a pedestrian crossing, a minimum clear distance of 200 cm shall be maintained between the crossing and the reserved space to improve visibility.  
  - The minimum dimensions of reserved spaces shall be:  
    - 600 cm long x 360 cm wide for parallel parking.  
    - 500 cm long x 360 cm wide for perpendicular parking (where a length of 600 cm is not possible).  
  - In constrained parallel parking, the width shall be no less than that of standard spaces and the reserved spaces shall be located adjacent to pedestrian crossings.  
  - Use of reserved spaces shall require proof of entitlement by means of an official document or card.  
- [ ] Isolated public toilets in open spaces shall be accessible to persons with reduced mobility, with at least one toilet per sex for every 10 units or fraction thereof.  
- [ ] Within the toilet layout, it shall be possible to inscribe an obstacle-free circle 180 cm in diameter.  
- [ ] The minimum clear passage width through doors and accessible toilet cubicles shall be 90 cm.  
- [ ] Doors shall have a protective plinth at least 30 cm high across their full width and shall open outwards.  
- [ ] Automatically opening doors shall have visual and audible warning signals.  
- [ ] Lighting, colour contrast and signage shall comply with Annex IV.  
- [ ] In banks of urinals, at least one shall be installed 45 cm above the floor, without pedestals or raised edges.  
- [ ] Street furniture shall be arranged without obstructing accessibility, aligned longitudinally along the pedestrian route and preferably at the outer edge of pavements, without reducing the clear width below 200 cm (or 150 cm in developments with ≤ 12 dwellings/ha).  
- [ ] Projecting façade or route elements (canopies, kiosks, awnings, signs, etc.) shall have their lower edge at a minimum height of 220 cm.  
- [ ] Fixed or movable elements located below 220 cm shall extend down to the ground over the greatest possible projection.  
- [ ] Transparent elements shall have two horizontal warning bands 20 cm wide, placed at heights of 90 and 150 cm above the ground, so they are perceptible to persons with visual impairments.  
- [ ] Bus stops and shelters shall provide, along one side and in the waiting area, an obstacle-free strip at least 180 cm wide.  
- [ ] Where seating is provided, it shall comply with the heights specified for accessible furniture.  
- [ ] Route and timetable information at stops shall comply with Annex IV on accessibility in communication, including identification of accessible buses.  
- [ ] Every bus stop shall be close to a pedestrian crossing point.  
- [ ] Where boarding takes place from a platform extending from the pavement, the platform shall have the same paving and characteristics as the pavement and may have a 20 cm kerb to reduce the height difference with the bus.  
- [ ] Shelters shall comply with the signage requirements for transparent elements.  
- [ ] External open spaces around the building shall comply with the technical accessibility requirements for the urban environment (Annex II).  
- [ ] Lifts shall comply with the applicable specific regulations (Royal Decree 1314/1997 and EN 81 standards), including accessibility for persons with disabilities.  
- [ ] Lift signage shall include pictograms, large print and labels in accordance with Annex IV.  
- [ ] General-interest rooms and public-service areas shall be laid out to allow a wheelchair user to remain and turn within them (in accordance with Annex I).  
- [ ] Public-assembly rooms with seating shall provide:  
  - Independent access to the platform or stage.  
  - Spaces reserved for persons with reduced mobility: at least 2 wheelchair spaces and 2 seats for users of walking aids for every 100 seats or fraction thereof.  
  - Minimum dimensions of reserved spaces: 140 cm x 110 cm, free of obstacles.  
  - Reserved seats 45 cm high, without a return mechanism, with folding armrests 20 cm above the seat.  
  - Reserved spaces preferably adjacent to aisles.  
  - Access aisles to reserved spaces and the platform at least 180 cm wide and with a gradient ≤ 6%.  
- [ ] In stadiums and grandstands, wheelchair spaces and an equivalent number of seats shall be reserved according to capacity, in areas close to entrances.  
- [ ] Protective guardrails shall be installed on raised platforms or at level differences greater than 40 cm, in accordance with the handrail specifications.  
- [ ] Furniture in public-use rooms and facilities shall comply with the anthropometric parameters for persons with reduced mobility (Annex I).  
- [ ] Furniture shall preferably be aligned on one side, with main passages 180 cm wide between items; edges and corners shall be rounded, the design shall be usable by wheelchair users, and it shall not create difficulties for cane users or persons with visual impairments.  
- [ ] Furniture shall be easy to locate, well illuminated and detectable with a mobility cane.  
- [ ] In waiting areas with seating, seats shall be located outside circulation areas, with a clear aisle connecting entrances and facilities.  
- [ ] Where seats are arranged in rows, the minimum distance between rows shall be 90 cm.  
- [ ] At least one seat in waiting areas shall be 45 cm high and have a folding armrest 20 cm above the seat.  
- [ ] In garages or car parks, at least one accessible space shall be reserved for every 40 spaces or fraction thereof, or one space for each reserved dwelling in residential buildings.  
- [ ] In tourist accommodation with parking service, one accessible parking space shall be reserved for each reserved accommodation unit.  
- [ ] Reserved spaces on roads or in open spaces shall comply with Annex II, point 3.11.  
- [ ] Reserved spaces inside buildings shall preferably be located on floors at street level and next to pedestrian entrances.  
- [ ] In residential buildings, dwellings shall be identified by signs displaying the number or letter, positioned perpendicular to the doorbell at a height between 150 and 160 cm.  
- [ ] Doors to storage rooms, common elements, garages and ancillary rooms shall also be identified by information signs complying with the accessibility-in-communication requirements of Annex IV.  
- [ ] In tourist accommodation, one accessible accommodation unit shall be reserved for every 50 units or fraction thereof, and one in every ten shall include assistive devices for persons with communication difficulties.  
- [ ] Reserved bedrooms and bathrooms shall comply with the requirements for dwellings intended for wheelchair users.  
- [ ] Bedrooms reserved for persons with communication difficulties shall have a visual doorbell and a call receiver in every room, including the bathroom.  
- [ ] Residential buildings other than dwellings and tourist accommodation shall establish reservations under conditions equivalent to those for tourist accommodation.  
- [ ] In large public buildings and open spaces, raised-relief plans and models shall be installed to aid orientation for persons with visual impairments. They shall be located in the main entrance hall or access areas, with raised and colour-contrasting text at a height between 90 and 120 cm.  
- [ ] In large buildings, tactile directional guidance strips at least 100 cm wide shall be used, with continuous, uninterrupted, slip-resistant paving of contrasting texture and colour, beginning in the access area and leading to points of interest.  
- [ ] Different textures should be used in guidance strips at intersections to facilitate orientation.  
- [ ] Tactile orientation plates shall be installed in large buildings with multiple route options, placed on the inner side edge of the handrail and providing information in raised lettering and Braille.

Cluster 5

- [ ] The project shall specify the minimum floor slip-resistance class according to its location and conditions (dry/wet interior, gradient, stairways, exterior, swimming pools and showers), in accordance with the class table based on the Rd (PTV) value obtained under UNE 41901:2017 EX.  
- [ ] The sample used for slip-resistance testing shall be representative of the most adverse flooring conditions.  
- [ ] The project shall ensure that the slip-resistance class is maintained throughout the service life of the flooring.  
- [ ] In areas that are neither restricted-use nor external, flooring joints shall not have raised edges exceeding 0.4 cm.  
- [ ] Local projecting elements (e.g. door strike plates) shall not project more than 1.2 cm above the flooring.  
- [ ] If a projection exceeds 0.6 cm on the face opposing the direction of travel, its angle with the flooring shall not exceed 45°.  
- [ ] Level differences of 5 cm or less shall be overcome using gradients no greater than 25%.  
- [ ] In circulation areas, flooring shall not contain perforations or openings through which a sphere 1.5 cm in diameter can pass.  
- [ ] Barriers delimiting circulation areas shall be at least 80 cm high.  
- [ ] Isolated steps or two consecutive steps shall not be provided in circulation areas, except in:  
  - [ ] Restricted-use areas.  
  - [ ] Common areas of residential housing buildings.  
  - [ ] Building entrances and exits.  
  - [ ] Access to platforms or stages.  
- [ ] Where steps exist in the areas listed above and an accessible route is available, the steps shall not be located on that route.  
- [ ] At level differences, voids or openings with a difference in level greater than 55 cm, protective barriers shall be installed unless the construction makes a fall unlikely or the barrier is incompatible with the use.  
- [ ] In public-use areas, level differences of 55 cm or less that could cause falls shall be marked by visual and tactile differentiation beginning at least 25 cm before the edge.  
- [ ] Glazed surfaces in impact-risk areas (doors between floor level and 150 cm high, plus 30 cm width on each side; fixed panes between floor level and 90 cm high) shall be made of laminated or toughened glass capable of withstanding a Level 3 impact under UNE-EN 12600:2003.  
- [ ] Glass whose largest dimension does not exceed 30 cm is exempt from this requirement.  
- [ ] The project shall include a lighting installation that ensures:  
  - [ ] Minimum illuminance of 100 lux in indoor areas (except indoor car parks).  
  - [ ] Minimum illuminance of 50 lux in indoor car parks.  
  - [ ] Minimum illuminance of 20 lux in external areas.  
  - [ ] Minimum average uniformity factor of 40%.  
- [ ] In public-assembly establishments with low-light activities (cinemas, theatres, auditoriums, nightclubs, etc.), marker lighting shall be provided on ramps and stair steps.  
- [ ] The emergency lighting installation shall be fixed, have its own power source, and activate automatically upon power failure (voltage below 70% of nominal).  
- [ ] Emergency lighting on evacuation routes shall achieve:  
  - [ ] At least 50% of the required level within 5 seconds.  
  - [ ] 100% of the required level within 60 seconds.  
- [ ] The emergency lighting installation shall guarantee at least one hour of operation after a failure, with:  
  - [ ] Minimum horizontal illuminance of 1 lux along the centreline and 0.5 lux in the central strip (at least half the width) for evacuation routes up to 200 cm wide.  
  - [ ] Wider routes may be divided into strips no more than 200 cm wide.  
  - [ ] Minimum illuminance of 5 lux at points containing safety equipment, manual fire-protection equipment and distribution boards.  
  - [ ] Maximum ratio of 40:1 between maximum and minimum illuminance along the centreline of the evacuation route.  
  - [ ] A maintenance factor and zero reflection from walls and ceilings shall be assumed when calculating levels.  
  - [ ] Lamps shall have a minimum colour rendering index Ra of 40 to ensure correct identification of safety colours.  
- [ ] The project shall include an assessment of the need for a lightning-protection system, determining:  
  - [ ] Expected strike frequency (Ne) according to strike density, equivalent collection area and environmental coefficient.  
  - [ ] Acceptable risk (Na) according to coefficients for construction type, contents, use and continuity requirements.  
- [ ] A lightning-protection system shall be installed when Ne > Na.  
- [ ] Buildings where toxic, radioactive, flammable or explosive substances are handled, and buildings over 43 m high, shall always have a lightning-protection system with efficiency E ≥ 0.98.  
- [ ] The required efficiency of the lightning-protection system shall be calculated using the regulatory formula and correspond to the protection level stated in the relevant table.  
- [ ] If the required efficiency falls within the limits exempting installation, lightning protection shall not be mandatory.
